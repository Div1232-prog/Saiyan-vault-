The idea behind the lab

A little distance can open a new angle.

“What would I advise a trusted friend facing the same situation?”

What is Solomon's paradox?

Solomon's paradox describes a possible gap between how wisely people reason about someone else's problem and how they reason about their own. When a decision feels personal, it can be harder to notice assumptions, trade-offs, or alternative perspectives.

Psychological distance is a way of mentally stepping back. Imagining that a trusted friend faces the same facts—or briefly referring to yourself by name—may help you examine the situation from another angle.

This is not about ignoring emotion. Feelings can point to needs, values, risks, or past experiences. Consider them alongside evidence, responsibilities, uncertainty, and the likely consequences of each option.

What the evidence supports: A 2014 paper reported three experiments on wise reasoning about close-relationship conflicts. Self-distancing instructions reduced the self–other asymmetry in two studies. This does not show that a worksheet guarantees a better outcome or works for every kind of decision.

Grossmann & Kross (2014) Moser et al. (2017)

How to use the lab

Move from a tangled choice to a useful next step.

Start small and work at your own pace. You do not need perfect certainty to make a thoughtful next move.

01

Describe the decision

State the choice, deadline, constraints, and who may be affected.

02

Create some distance

Imagine a trusted friend facing the same facts. What would you advise?

03

Compare real options

Consider benefits, costs, risks, unknowns, and longer-term effects.

04

Choose a next step

Identify one practical action that fits your priorities and constraints.

05

Review and learn

Set a review point, notice what changed, and adjust without self-judgment.

The method is a thinking aid, not a test you can pass or fail. Emotions, evidence, values, and trade-offs all deserve a place in the process.

A quick note on responsible use

Before You Begin: Important Precautions

Protect your privacy, check important claims, and keep responsibility for your decisions in your own hands.

  • Never share passwords, one-time passwords (OTPs), authentication codes, banking credentials, or unnecessary sensitive personal information with an AI tool.
  • Avoid private identifying details. Describe your situation generally whenever possible.
  • AI responses may contain errors or assumptions. Verify important information independently.
  • Use AI as a thinking partner, not as an authority that makes decisions for you.
  • AI services have different privacy and data-retention policies. Do not assume your conversations are confidential.
  • For high-stakes medical, legal, financial, or safety matters, consult an appropriately qualified professional.
  • For emergencies or immediate safety concerns, seek prompt help from local emergency services or an appropriate trusted person.
  • This toolkit is for educational and reflective purposes. It is not a substitute for professional advice.
✳Your decision remains yours. The goal is to organize your thinking, make uncertainty visible, and identify a reasonable next step—not to remove emotion or promise certainty.

A few useful answers

Questions before you begin?

Use the toolkit as a flexible aid. Adapt the pace to the decision and the support it may require.

What is The Solomon's Decision Lab?

It is a three-resource toolkit inspired by research on Solomon's paradox. It helps you reflect on a personal decision, consider another perspective, compare realistic options, and identify a practical next step. It is not a validated standalone intervention or a guarantee of better decisions.

Which resource should I use first?

Start with the two-page Quick-Start Guide to understand the process and its limits. Use the one-page Worksheet to write through a decision. The AI Thinking-Partner Prompt is optional and can help structure a conversation with an AI service.

Can I use the AI prompt with different AI services?

The prompt is plain text, so you can try it in AI services that accept pasted text. Responses, features, limits, and compatibility vary by service. Review the service's current rules and privacy policy before sharing information.

Should I share sensitive information?

No. Do not enter passwords, one-time passwords, authentication codes, banking credentials, or unnecessary identifying or sensitive details. Keep examples general wherever possible and do not assume that an AI conversation is confidential.

Does this framework guarantee the right decision?

No. It can organize reflection but cannot remove uncertainty, account for every relevant fact, or guarantee an outcome. Emotions can carry useful information; weigh them alongside evidence, values, constraints, and trade-offs. For high-stakes matters, seek qualified professional advice.
